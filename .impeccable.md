# Halve — Impeccable Design Context

## Design Context

### Users
Dedicated daily-puzzle players — the person who already opens Wordle, Connections, and the mini crossword every morning. They are fluent in the daily-puzzle ritual and don't need to be convinced to play; they need the app to respect their time and intelligence. They come back because the *solve* feels good, not because the app nudges or gamifies them. Mobile-first; likely plays in a quiet moment (morning commute, lunch, before bed).

### Brand Personality
**Quiet. Earned. Elemental.**

The brand speaks like a calm host, never a hype-man. It trusts the player completely. It explains the rules once, clearly, then gets entirely out of the way. The emotional arc is tension → deduction → resolution — not excitement → reward → dopamine loop. The two game symbols (solid circle and ring) do all the iconic and emotional heavy lifting; the UI chrome exists only to serve them.

Voice rules: sentence case everywhere. Short sentences. No exclamation marks except genuine celebration (sparingly). No "crush it", "level up", "unlock", "addictive". No emoji. Middle dots `·` and em dashes `—` for metadata and asides.

### Aesthetic Direction
**Refined minimalism** — the calm, premium feel of Things or Linear, applied to a puzzle game.

- Warm paper/ink duality is the signature device: `#F6F1E8` (paper) / `#14110B` (ink). Neutrals are warm, never cold grey.
- Single accent: amber-red (`#B03E12` light / `#E86B38` dark) — the solid symbol (A) colour. Teal is the ring symbol (B) colour, not a UI accent.
- Space Grotesk exclusively — weight 300–700, display/headings at 500 with `-0.02em` tracking. No Inter, no second typeface.
- 8pt spacing grid. Generous whitespace. Screens breathe.
- The 6×6 puzzle grid is the hero of every game screen. Everything else is chrome.
- Flat warm solids — no gradients, no images, no glassmorphism, no textures.
- Restrained radii: `6 / 10 / 12 (cell) / 16 / 22`. Primary buttons are pills.
- Motion is tactile and confirming, never decorative. ~160–220ms, `cubic-bezier(0.4,0,0.2,1)`. No bounces, no looping ambient.

**Anti-references:** not AI slop (no purple/blue gradients, no everything-centered layouts, no default Inter, no generic card grids), and not the NYT Games aesthetic (bold block colours, heavy gamification chrome, emoji-heavy share text, congratulatory banners).

### Design Principles

1. **The puzzle is the reward.** Satisfaction comes from the correctly-filled board, not from the app congratulating you. Win states, streaks, and stats carry quiet weight — no confetti, no badges, no level-up language. The silence after a solve is part of the design.

2. **Restraint as a signal of quality.** Every element on screen must earn its place. Decorative copy, filler microcopy ("Start your streak today"), and symmetrical-for-symmetry's-sake layouts are red flags. Cut before adding. Asymmetry over rigid centering.

3. **Deductive satisfaction, expressed quietly.** Parity's differentiator is provable deduction — every cell is logically certain. The design should honour that without overselling it. The solve time and streak are shown with clean typographic weight. The board itself — fully resolved — is the visual payoff.

4. **Warm, not cosy.** The palette is warm but the tone is not folksy or cute. It's closer to a well-made hardcover book than a coffee-shop loyalty card. Warmth comes from the materials (paper, ink, amber fire, cool water), not from friendliness gestures.

5. **Chrome serves the grid.** Navigation, stats, auth, and home all exist to put the player in front of the 6×6 grid as cleanly as possible. Hierarchy on every non-board screen should be ruthless: one primary action, secondary options clearly subordinate, nothing competing for attention.
